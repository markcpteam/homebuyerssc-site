Before accepting any cash offer in Charleston, verify three things: that the buyer is purchasing the house themselves rather than assigning your contract to someone else, that they can show proof of funds, and that the closing will be handled by a South Carolina closing attorney. Those three checks separate real buyers from lead resellers, and all three can be confirmed in a single phone call.

## What is actually behind a "we buy houses" sign?

Three different kinds of business use the same marketing, and they are not the same thing.

| Type | What they actually do | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct buyer** | Buys the house with their own funds, holds or renovates it, resells it later | One party, one price, they control whether the deal closes |
| **Wholesaler** | Puts your house under contract, then sells that contract to a third party for a fee | The real buyer is someone you have not met. Deals fall through when no one takes the assignment |
| **Lead reseller** | Collects your information and sells it as a lead to several investors | Your phone starts ringing. Nobody has committed to buying anything |

None of these is illegal. Wholesaling is a legitimate business. The problem is being told you are dealing with one and actually dealing with another.

## What questions should I ask a cash home buyer?

Ask these five, in this order. The first one matters most.

1. **"Will you be the buyer on the contract, closing in your own name, or do you intend to assign this contract to someone else?"** A direct buyer answers immediately and without qualification. A wholesaler talks about their "network of buyers" or their "investor list." Both answers are useful, so long as you get a straight one.
2. **"Can you send proof of funds?"** A bank statement or a letter from a financial institution showing they hold the money. A real buyer has this ready.
3. **"Which closing attorney will you use?"** More on why this matters below.
4. **"What is your offer based on?"** A real buyer can walk you through the after-repair value, the estimated repair cost, and what is left. If the number appears with no explanation, ask for one.
5. **"What happens if you change your mind?"** Ask what the contract says about the buyer walking away, and how long your house is tied up if they do.

## Why does the closing attorney matter in South Carolina?

Because South Carolina is one of the states where it is not optional.

In **State v. Buyers Service Co.**, 292 S.C. 426, 357 S.E.2d 15 (1987), the South Carolina Supreme Court held that real estate closings must be conducted under the supervision of a licensed attorney. The court's reasoning was consumer protection: a non-lawyer running a closing might be "tempted to offer a few words of explanation, however innocent, rather than risk losing a fee."

What this means practically: a buyer who talks about using an out-of-state title company, or who is vague about who is handling the closing, is either unfamiliar with how South Carolina works or is routing you somewhere you should ask about. Established local buyers use established local closing attorneys.

*The statutes cited here are the starting point, not the whole answer. How they apply turns on your specific facts, so confirm with a licensed South Carolina attorney before acting.*

## What are the actual warning signs?

- **Pressure to sign today.** A real offer is still a real offer tomorrow.
- **The offer changes after inspection, with no explanation.** Renegotiating on something genuinely discovered is normal. A pattern of dropping the price late is not.
- **A large non-refundable deposit requested from you.** You are the seller. You should not be paying the buyer.
- **No physical address, or an address in another state with no local presence.**
- **Vagueness about who is buying.** See question one.
- **Requests to sign a contract before you have seen the whole thing**, including what happens if they walk.

## Is a cash offer worth less than listing?

Usually, yes, and any buyer telling you otherwise is not being straight with you.

A cash offer is below what a renovated house would fetch on the open market. That difference is what you trade for speed, no repairs, no showings, no commission, and certainty that the sale closes. Whether that trade is worth it depends entirely on your situation.

It is often worth it if the house needs work you cannot fund, if you are on a deadline, if the property is tenant-occupied or inherited, or if a failed listing has already cost you months. It is usually not worth it if the house is in good condition, you are not in a hurry, and your local market is moving. In that case, list it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are "we buy houses" companies in Charleston legitimate?**

Most are real businesses. The important distinction is not legitimate versus scam, it is direct buyer versus wholesaler versus lead reseller. Ask which one you are talking to.

**How do I know the offer is fair?**

Ask the buyer to show how they got to the number: what they think the house is worth renovated, what they estimate the repairs cost, and what margin they are keeping. A buyer who will not show that arithmetic is asking you to trust a number you cannot check. You can also get a second cash offer, and a real buyer will not object to that.

**Should I get more than one cash offer?**

Yes. Any buyer who discourages you from doing so has told you something about themselves.

**Do I have to pay anything?**

No. On a straightforward cash purchase the buyer covers closing costs and there is no commission. If you are asked to pay a fee to receive an offer, stop.

**What if my house has a tenant, code violations, or is in probate?**

Those are common and do not prevent a sale, though they change the timeline.
