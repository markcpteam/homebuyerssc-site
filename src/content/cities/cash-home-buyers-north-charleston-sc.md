Homebuyers SC buys houses in North Charleston as-is for cash, with no repairs, no showings, no commission, and a closing date you choose.

North Charleston spans three counties, so the first thing to establish about any property here is which county it actually sits in. That single fact decides which probate court, which deeds office, and which delinquent tax office applies to you.

## Which county is your North Charleston property in?

The county decides which offices you deal with, and on some North Charleston addresses the answer is not obvious.

| What you need | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| Which county the parcel is in | **Establish this first.** Charleston, Berkeley and Dorchester all contain part of this area. Your property tax notice shows it |
| Probate, if the owner has died | The probate court of that county |
| Recorded deeds, liens, judgments | That county's Register of Deeds |
| Delinquent property taxes | That county's delinquent tax office |

**Insurance note.** This matters more than usual here. Eligibility for the South Carolina Wind and Hail Underwriting Association, the state's coastal wind and hail market of last resort, follows designated zones in Charleston County and does not extend to Berkeley or Dorchester. Two properties a few miles apart can face different insurance markets.

## Should you sell to a cash buyer at all?

Often not. If the house is in good condition, a lender would finance it today, and you have time to wait, listing it will usually net you more. We would rather tell you that than write an offer you should not take. The arithmetic, including the case for listing, is in [cash offer vs listing with an agent in Charleston](/guides/cash-offer-vs-realtor-what-you-net-in-south-carolina/).

A cash sale tends to fit when the house needs work a lender will not accept, you are against a deadline, the property is tenant-occupied or in an estate, or a listing has already failed.

## Questions we get about North Charleston property

**How do I find out which county my property is in?**

Your property tax notice shows the county. It matters because probate, recorded deeds, and delinquent taxes all go through that county's offices, and North Charleston spans three.

**Does the county change my insurance situation?**

It can. The state wind and hail program's designated zones are in Charleston County, not Berkeley or Dorchester. Confirm for your address with an agent.

**There is an open code case on the property. Can I still sell?**

Yes. Citations follow the property, and accrued penalties are dealt with at closing. Get the written total from code enforcement before you talk to buyers.
