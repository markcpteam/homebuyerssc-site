## Which county is your Charleston property in?

The county decides which offices you deal with, and on some Charleston addresses the answer is not obvious.

| What you need | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| Probate, if the owner has died | Charleston County Probate Court |
| Recorded deeds, liens, judgments | Charleston County Register of Deeds |
| Delinquent property taxes | Charleston County delinquent tax office |

**Insurance note.** The South Carolina Wind and Hail Underwriting Association, the state's market of last resort for wind and hail coverage, operates in designated coastal zones of Charleston County. It covers wind and hail only, not fire, liability, or flood, and it carries condition requirements including a sound roof. Confirm eligibility for your address with an insurance agent.

## What comes up most on Charleston houses

- **Historic review.** Large parts of the peninsula sit inside districts where exterior alterations and demolition are controlled. That affects what any buyer can do with the property, and it is worth knowing before you price it.
- **Pier-and-beam over crawlspaces.** The most common finding in older peninsula and downtown housing is moisture and failed piers under the floor, not anything visible from the street.
- **Flood.** Much of the peninsula sits low. The flood zone and any elevation certificate change what a financed buyer can insure and borrow.
- **Short-term rental rules** are specific and enforced here. If the property has been used that way, get the current rule in writing from the city.

One walkthrough, a written offer with proof of funds usually within 24 to 48 hours, then an attorney-supervised closing on a date you pick, typically one to three weeks once title clears, or up to twelve months out if that suits you better. ## Should you sell to a cash buyer at all?

Often not. If the house is in good condition, a lender would finance it today, and you have time to wait, listing it will usually net you more. We would rather tell you that than write an offer you should not take. The arithmetic, including the case for listing, is in [cash offer vs listing with an agent in Charleston](/guides/cash-offer-vs-realtor-what-you-net-in-south-carolina/).

A cash sale tends to fit when the house needs work a lender will not accept, you are against a deadline, the property is tenant-occupied or in an estate, or a listing has already failed.

## Questions we get about Charleston property

**Does the historic district affect what you will pay?**

It affects what any buyer can do with the property, so yes, it is part of the calculation. It is also a reason to establish early exactly what review applies to your address, because assumptions in both directions are common.

**The house is on a crawlspace and I have never been under it. Is that a problem?**

It is the most common unknown on older Charleston housing and we price it either way. If you have never looked, say so rather than guessing. Access for us to look is worth more than a description.

**What if the property floods?**

Flood is separate coverage from homeowners, and the zone and any elevation certificate decide what a financed buyer can insure. We buy in flood-prone areas.
