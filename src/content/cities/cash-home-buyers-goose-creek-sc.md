## Which county is your Goose Creek property in?

The county decides which offices you deal with, and on some Goose Creek addresses the answer is not obvious.

| What you need | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| Probate, if the owner has died | Berkeley County Probate Court |
| Recorded deeds, liens, judgments | Berkeley County Register of Deeds |
| Delinquent property taxes | Berkeley County delinquent tax office |

**Insurance note.** Berkeley County sits outside the designated counties for the South Carolina Wind and Hail Underwriting Association, the state's coastal wind and hail market of last resort. Coverage is not automatic even so: roof age, condition, and claims history decide it, and flood is always separate.

## What comes up most on Goose Creek houses

- **Not in the coastal wind pool.** The state wind and hail program covers designated coastal zones in Charleston, Beaufort, Colleton, Georgetown, and Horry counties. Berkeley County is not among them, so the coastal wind question that dominates island sales does not apply the same way here.
- **Largely subdivision housing**, much of it slab-on-grade and newer than the coastal stock, which usually means narrower condition findings.
- **Berkeley County offices** handle probate, deeds, and delinquent taxes. City of Goose Creek handles permits and code within city limits.
- **Flood still matters** on specific parcels near creeks and low ground. Zone, not city, decides it.

## The process, briefly

One walkthrough, a written offer with proof of funds usually within 24 to 48 hours, then an attorney-supervised closing on a date you pick, typically one to three weeks once title clears, or up to twelve months out if that suits you better. South Carolina requires a licensed attorney to supervise every closing, under *State v. Buyers Service Co.*, 292 S.C. 426, 357 S.E.2d 15 (1987).

Full detail, including what sets the timeline and what you are never asked to do, is in [how selling to a cash home buyer works in South Carolina](/guides/how-selling-to-a-cash-home-buyer-works-in-south-carolina/).

## Should you sell to a cash buyer at all?

Often not. If the house is in good condition, a lender would finance it today, and you have time to wait, listing it will usually net you more. We would rather tell you that than write an offer you should not take. The arithmetic, including the case for listing, is in [cash offer vs listing with an agent in Charleston](/guides/cash-offer-vs-realtor-what-you-net-in-south-carolina/).

A cash sale tends to fit when the house needs work a lender will not accept, you are against a deadline, the property is tenant-occupied or in an estate, or a listing has already failed.
