Moncks Corner is the seat of Berkeley County, which makes one part of selling here simpler than almost anywhere else in the tri-county area: the offices you need are in town rather than an hour away.

## Which county is your Moncks Corner property in?

Berkeley County, and the county government is right here.

| What you need | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| Probate, if the owner has died | Berkeley County Probate Court, in Moncks Corner |
| Recorded deeds, liens, judgments | Berkeley County Register of Deeds, in Moncks Corner |
| Delinquent property taxes | Berkeley County delinquent tax office, in Moncks Corner |
| Permit history for the address | Town of Moncks Corner, or Berkeley County for unincorporated parcels |

That matters more than it sounds. Pulling a permit record, checking what is recorded against a property, or finding out where a probate file stands are all things you can do in person in an afternoon here, rather than driving to Charleston.

**Insurance note.** Berkeley County sits outside the designated counties for the South Carolina Wind and Hail Underwriting Association, the state's coastal wind and hail market of last resort. Coverage is not automatic even so: roof age, condition, and claims history decide it, and flood is always separate.

## What comes up most on Moncks Corner houses

- **Water proximity.** The town sits south of Lake Moultrie, with the West Branch of the Cooper River on its eastern side. Flood zone is a parcel-level question here, not a town-level one, and it decides what a financed buyer can insure.
- **Mixed housing ages.** Older housing in and around the town core, newer subdivision construction further out. The condition findings differ sharply between the two, and roof age is the most common insurance trigger on the older stock.
- **Rural parcels outside town.** Private wells and septic systems are common. A failing septic is a condition issue a lender will not overlook, and it is not visible from a walkthrough.
- **Family land.** Property held across generations without probate turns up in rural Berkeley County, and it is the most likely reason a sale here stalls. See the heirs property guide below.

## The process, briefly

One walkthrough, a written offer with proof of funds usually within 24 to 48 hours, then an attorney-supervised closing on a date you pick. Once the closing attorney confirms clear title, the closing itself is usually one to three weeks away, and you can set it further out if that suits you, up to twelve months. South Carolina requires a licensed attorney to supervise every closing, under *State v. Buyers Service Co.*, 292 S.C. 426, 357 S.E.2d 15 (1987).

## Should you sell to a cash buyer at all?

Often not. If the house is in good condition, a lender would finance it today, and you have time to wait, listing it will usually net you more. We would rather tell you that than write an offer you should not take. The arithmetic, including the case for listing, is in [cash offer vs listing with an agent in Charleston](/guides/cash-offer-vs-realtor-what-you-net-in-south-carolina/).

A cash sale tends to fit when the house needs work a lender will not accept, you are against a deadline, the property is tenant-occupied or in an estate, or a listing has already failed.

**Everything county is right here. Does that speed anything up?**

It speeds up you, not the closing. Pulling a permit record, checking recorded liens, or asking about a probate file is a short trip rather than a day. The closing timeline is still set by title work.

**The property is on a well and septic. Is that a problem?**

Not for us. It matters to a lender, and a failing septic is expensive and invisible. Tell us what you know, including if the answer is nothing.

**The land has been in the family a long time and the paperwork is unclear.**

Take that to an attorney before doing anything else. Undocumented family ownership is the most common reason a rural sale stalls, and South Carolina's heirs property law shapes what happens next.
