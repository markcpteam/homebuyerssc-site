## Which county is your Edisto property in?

The county decides which offices you deal with, and on some Edisto addresses the answer is not obvious.

| What you need | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| Which county the parcel is in | **Establish this first.** Colleton and Charleston all contain part of this area. Your property tax notice shows it |
| Probate, if the owner has died | The probate court of that county |
| Recorded deeds, liens, judgments | That county's Register of Deeds |
| Delinquent property taxes | That county's delinquent tax office |

**Insurance note.** The South Carolina Wind and Hail Underwriting Association, the state's market of last resort for wind and hail coverage, operates in designated coastal zones of Colleton County and Charleston County. It covers wind and hail only, not fire, liability, or flood, and it carries condition requirements including a sound roof. Confirm eligibility for your address with an insurance agent.

## What comes up most on Edisto houses

- **Two counties.** Edisto Beach is Colleton County. The majority of Edisto Island is unincorporated Charleston County. Which one your parcel is in decides the probate court, the deeds office, and the tax office.
- **Both counties are in the coastal wind pool territory**, so the wind and hail question applies here either way, alongside separate flood coverage.
- **Barrier island exposure.** Salt, wind, and moisture drive the condition findings: roof, envelope, decks, pilings, and framing.
- **Distance from services.** Contractors, inspectors, and even walkthroughs take longer to arrange here than in town.

One walkthrough, a written offer with proof of funds usually within 24 to 48 hours, then an attorney-supervised closing on a date you pick, typically one to three weeks once title clears, or up to twelve months out if that suits you better. ## Should you sell to a cash buyer at all?

Often not. If the house is in good condition, a lender would finance it today, and you have time to wait, listing it will usually net you more. We would rather tell you that than write an offer you should not take. The arithmetic, including the case for listing, is in [cash offer vs listing with an agent in Charleston](/guides/cash-offer-vs-realtor-what-you-net-in-south-carolina/).

A cash sale tends to fit when the house needs work a lender will not accept, you are against a deadline, the property is tenant-occupied or in an estate, or a listing has already failed.
