Homebuyers SC buys houses in Walterboro as-is for cash, with no repairs, no showings, no commission, and a closing date you choose.

Walterboro is the seat of Colleton County, about 48 miles west of Charleston. Two things make selling here different from the rest of the Lowcountry: the county offices you need are in town, and despite being well inland, Colleton is one of the counties where the state's coastal wind and hail program operates.

## Which county is your Walterboro property in?

Colleton County, and the county government is right here.

| What you need | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| Probate, if the owner has died | Colleton County Probate Court, in Walterboro |
| Recorded deeds, liens, judgments | Colleton County Register of Deeds, in Walterboro |
| Delinquent property taxes | Colleton County delinquent tax office, in Walterboro |
| Permit history for the address | City of Walterboro, or Colleton County for unincorporated parcels |

This matters for anyone dealing with an estate. If the owner lived in Colleton County, the probate file is here, not in Charleston, and using the wrong county's court costs time you may not have.

**Insurance note.** Colleton County is one of the designated counties for the South Carolina Wind and Hail Underwriting Association, the state's market of last resort for wind and hail coverage, alongside Beaufort, Charleston, Georgetown, and Horry. Eligibility follows designated coastal zones rather than the whole county, it covers wind and hail only and not fire, liability, or flood, and it carries condition requirements including a sound roof. Confirm for your specific address with an insurance agent.

## What comes up most on Walterboro houses

- **Older housing stock** in and around the historic town core, with the pier-and-beam construction, crawlspace moisture, and roof age that go with it.
- **Rural parcels** across much of the county, frequently on private wells and septic systems. Both are condition items a lender cares about and neither is visible on a walkthrough.
- **Larger tracts and timber land**, where the recorded description and the ground do not always agree. A survey is often the cheapest way to remove uncertainty.
- **ACE Basin proximity.** Low ground, wetlands, and flood exposure are parcel-level questions here. Flood is separate coverage from homeowners regardless of what the wind and hail program does.
- **Distance from Charleston.** Contractors, inspectors, and even walkthroughs take longer to arrange, which is worth factoring into any timeline anyone promises you.

## The process, briefly

One walkthrough, a written offer with proof of funds usually within 24 to 48 hours, then an attorney-supervised closing on a date you pick. Once the closing attorney confirms clear title, the closing itself is usually one to three weeks away, and you can set it further out if that suits you, up to twelve months. ## Should you sell to a cash buyer at all?

Often not. If the house is in good condition, a lender would finance it today, and you have time to wait, listing it will usually net you more. We would rather tell you that than write an offer you should not take. The arithmetic, including the case for listing, is in [cash offer vs listing with an agent in Charleston](/guides/cash-offer-vs-realtor-what-you-net-in-south-carolina/).

A cash sale tends to fit when the house needs work a lender will not accept, you are against a deadline, the property is tenant-occupied or in an estate, or a listing has already failed.

**Is Walterboro really in the coastal wind pool?**

Colleton is one of the designated counties for the state wind and hail program, which surprises people this far inland. Eligibility follows designated zones rather than the whole county, so confirm for your address rather than assuming either way.

**The property is on a well and septic. Does that stop a sale?**

Not to us. It matters to a lender, and a failing septic is both expensive and invisible. Say what you know.

**The parcel is large and the boundaries are unclear.**

Common on rural tracts. A survey is frequently the cheapest way to remove uncertainty, and uncertainty is what buyers discount.

**The owner died and lived in Colleton County. Where does probate happen?**

Colleton County Probate Court, here in Walterboro. Being appointed personal representative is not by itself enough to sell the house in South Carolina. See [selling an inherited house in probate in South Carolina](/guides/selling-an-inherited-house-in-probate-in-south-carolina/).
