You can close a cash sale in South Carolina in about 7 days from signed contract to funds in your account. The title search is usually what sets the pace. A financed sale adds 30 to 45 days of lender underwriting after you accept an offer, on top of however long the house sat on the market first.

Three things decide your actual timeline: whether the buyer is paying cash or borrowing, the condition of the house, and how quickly the closing attorney can clear title.

Homebuyers SC has closed sales in as few as 7 days across Charleston, North Charleston, Mount Pleasant, Summerville, and the surrounding Lowcountry.

## How long does each path actually take?

| Stage | Cash sale | Financed sale |
|---|---|---|
| Offer | 24 to 48 hours after a walkthrough | After listing, showings, and however long the house sits |
| Inspection and appraisal | Not required | 1 to 3 weeks, and either one can end the deal |
| Lender underwriting | None | 30 to 45 days minimum, longer if anything is unusual |
| Title search and clearance | 3 to 7 business days | 3 to 7 business days, run in parallel |
| Closing appointment | 30 to 60 minutes | 30 to 60 minutes |
| Funds | Same day, wired or certified check | Same day, after the lender funds |
| Realistic total | About 7 to 14 days | Days on market, plus 30 to 45 days |

The gap is not that cash buyers work faster. It is that an entire category of steps does not exist. No appraisal to come in low, no underwriter to raise a question two days before closing, no financing contingency to fall back on.

## What are the steps to a fast sale in South Carolina?

1. **Get a written offer with proof of funds.** A legitimate buyer can usually provide both within 24 to 48 hours of a walkthrough. If proof of funds does not come with the offer, ask why.
2. **Sign a clear contract.** A standard purchase agreement with no unusual contingencies can be signed the same day. Read every line, especially what happens if the buyer walks.
3. **Hand off to the closing attorney.** South Carolina requires that closings be supervised by a licensed attorney. That is settled law from *State v. Buyers Service Co.*, 292 S.C. 426, 357 S.E.2d 15 (1987), not local custom. The attorney orders title work and checks for liens and judgments.

*The statutes cited here are the starting point, not the whole answer. How they apply turns on your specific facts, so confirm with a licensed South Carolina attorney before acting.*
4. **Title search and clearance.** Usually 3 to 7 business days on a clean title. Old liens, missing heirs, or back taxes extend it.
5. **Set the closing date.** Most cash sales can close in 7 to 14 days. If you need longer, a real buyer can usually work to 30, 60, or 90 days out.
6. **Sign and get paid.** The appointment runs 30 to 60 minutes. Funds are wired or issued by certified check the same day.

## What slows a sale down in South Carolina?

Almost always title, and almost always something the seller knew about but did not mention.

- **Choosing a financed buyer when speed is the point.** Even a strong loan takes 30 to 45 days.
- **Underestimating title work.** Even a cash sale needs a few business days for the search.
- **Liens, back taxes, or unpaid utilities.** These have to be cleared before title transfers. In South Carolina unpaid sewer charges are a lien on the property, superior to everything but property taxes. See [selling a house with years of unpaid utilities](/guides/selling-a-house-with-years-of-unpaid-utilities/) and [selling a house with back taxes or liens](/guides/selling-a-house-with-back-taxes-or-liens-how-to-move-forward-without-paying-them-first/).
- **Probate.** A personal representative in South Carolina generally cannot sell estate real property without authority in the will or a petition to the probate court. This is the single biggest timeline variable on an inherited house. See [selling an inherited house in probate in South Carolina](/guides/selling-an-inherited-house-in-probate-in-south-carolina/).
- **Missing paperwork.** Photo ID, mortgage payoff, HOA contact, and the deed. Tracking these down mid-process costs days.
- **Tenants.** A lease survives the sale. See [selling a house with tenants in South Carolina](/guides/selling-a-house-with-tenants-in-south-carolina-a-landlords-guide/).
- **Holding out for a higher offer while carrying costs run.** Two extra months of mortgage, taxes, insurance, and utilities frequently exceeds the increase being chased.

## When does speed matter more than price?

Some situations do not allow for a listing period. A relocation with a hard start date, a settlement deadline in a [divorce or separation](/divorce-or-separation/), medical bills that will not wait, a house you can no longer afford to carry, or a property that no lender will finance in its current condition.

In those cases the few percentage points a longer listing might produce are usually consumed by carrying costs, court timelines, or a deal that falls through at inspection and starts the clock over. The arithmetic is in [cash offer vs listing with a realtor in South Carolina](/guides/cash-offer-vs-realtor-what-you-net-in-south-carolina/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

## What is the fastest I can realistically close in SC?

About 7 days from signed contract to funds. The title search is the gating factor, and most clean titles clear within a week.

## How long does title work take in South Carolina?

Usually 3 to 7 business days for a clean title. If problems surface, plan on an extra 1 to 4 weeks depending on what is needed to clear them. See [selling a house with title problems](/guides/selling-a-house-with-title-problems-how-to-close-when-ownership-isnt-clear/).

## Can I close from out of state?

Yes, and it is common. South Carolina does not currently permit remote online notarization, so you cannot sign over video. What you can do is sign in front of a notary physically present with you, wherever you live, and return the documents by overnight courier. The closing itself is still supervised by a South Carolina attorney. See [selling an inherited house when you live out of state](/guides/selling-an-inherited-property-out-of-state-without-the-hassle/).

## What documents do I need at closing?

A government-issued photo ID, your mortgage payoff statement (the attorney can request it), and anything bearing on ownership, such as an old deed, a divorce decree, or probate paperwork.

## Will closing fast hurt what I net?

Less than the headline difference suggests. Speed removes holding costs, commission, and the risk of a buyer falling through. Whether that offsets the lower offer depends on your situation, and an honest buyer will tell you when it does not.

## Is a cash sale faster than an iBuyer?

Usually. iBuyers add inspection windows, repair negotiations, and service fees. A local buyer can also move the closing date to fit your situation, which is harder to arrange with a national platform.

## What if there are liens or back taxes on the property?

The closing attorney pays them from the sale proceeds. You do not clear them yourself first. They come off your net rather than out of your pocket.

## How do I know the buyer can actually close in 7 days?

Ask for proof of funds and the name of the closing attorney before you sign anything. A buyer who cannot produce both is not a 7-day buyer. [How to choose a cash home buyer in Charleston](/how-to-choose-a-cash-home-buyer/) covers the rest.
