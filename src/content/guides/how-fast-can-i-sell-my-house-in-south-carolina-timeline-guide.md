## Quick Answer

You can sell a house in South Carolina in as little as 7 days with a cash buyer, while a traditional MLS listing typically takes 60 to 105 days from list date to funds in your account. The actual timeline depends on three things: the buyer's funding source (cash versus financed), the condition of your home, and how quickly the title work and closing attorney can wrap things up. Cash sales skip the appraisal, financing contingencies, and lender underwriting that slow most transactions to a crawl. If you need to move quickly and you're working with local [cash home buyers in North Charleston](https://homebuyerssc.com/cash-home-buyers-north-charleston-sc/), the timeline is largely under your control.

## The Steps to a Fast Sale in SC

  1. **Get a written cash offer with proof of funds.** A legitimate buyer can usually provide an offer within 24 to 48 hours of a quick walkthrough. Make sure proof of funds comes with it.

  2. **Sign a simple, clear contract.** A standard purchase agreement, free of unusual contingencies, can be signed the same day. Read every line first.

  3. **Hand off to the closing attorney.** South Carolina is an attorney-state, so every closing runs through a licensed real estate attorney. They'll order title work and check for liens or judgments.

  4. **Title search and clearance.** This usually takes 3 to 7 business days. Issues like old liens, missing heirs, or back taxes can extend it — knowing about them up front saves time later.

  5. **Schedule your closing date.** Most cash sales can close in 7 to 14 days. If you need more time, the buyer can usually accommodate 30, 60, or even 90 days out.

  6. **Sign final paperwork and get paid.** Closing usually takes 30 to 60 minutes. Funds are wired or issued by certified check the same day.

## Common Mistakes That Slow Things Down

  * **Choosing a financed buyer when speed matters.** Even a strong loan takes 30 to 45 days minimum. If you need to close fast, cash is the only realistic path.

  * **Underestimating the attorney and title timeline.** Even with a cash buyer, title work takes a few business days. Build it into your plan.

  * **Missing key paperwork.** Have your photo ID, mortgage payoff statement, HOA contact (if any), and deed handy. Tracking these down mid-process delays closing.

  * **Not knowing about liens or back taxes.** Old judgments, unpaid HOA dues, or property tax liens have to be resolved before clear title can transfer. Surface them early.

  * **Trying to coordinate same-day move-out without a plan.** If you'll still be in the home at closing, negotiate a post-closing possession period upfront.

  * **Holding out for a higher offer while carrying costs eat the difference.** Two extra months of mortgage, taxes, and utilities can cost more than the bump you're chasing.

## When a Fast Close Is the Priority

Some situations don't allow for a 90-day listing. A job relocation with a hard start date, a settlement deadline during a [divorce or separation](https://homebuyerssc.com/divorce-or-separation/), a medical bill that won't wait, or a property you can no longer afford to carry — all of these are cases where a cash sale isn't just convenient, it's necessary. In those situations, the few percentage points you'd theoretically gain from a longer listing are almost always wiped out by carrying costs, court timelines, or stress.

## Frequently Asked Questions

## What's the fastest I can realistically close on a home sale in SC?

About 7 days from signed contract to funds in your account. The title search is usually the gating factor, and most clean titles clear within a week.

## What slows down a cash sale?

The most common culprits are title issues (liens, judgments, missing heirs), back taxes, HOA payoff delays, or missing seller paperwork. Surfacing these early keeps the timeline tight.

## How long does title work take in South Carolina?

Usually 3 to 7 business days for a clean title. If issues come up, plan on an extra 1 to 4 weeks depending on what's needed to clear them.

## Can I close from out of state?

Yes. South Carolina allows remote online notarization and mail-away closings. You can sign and notarize documents from anywhere in the country with a notary present.

## What documents do I need at closing?

A government-issued photo ID, your mortgage payoff statement (the attorney can request it), and any documentation related to ownership — like an old deed, divorce decree, or probate paperwork.

## Will closing fast hurt what I net?

Not necessarily. Speed reduces holding costs, eliminates commissions, and removes the risk of buyer fall-through. For many sellers, those savings offset the slightly lower offer.

## Is a cash sale faster than an iBuyer like Opendoor?

Usually yes. iBuyers tack on inspection windows, repair negotiations, and service fees that local cash buyers don't. A local buyer can also adjust the timeline to your needs in a way iBuyers typically can't.

## What if there are liens or back taxes on the property?

The closing attorney pays them off out of the sale proceeds at closing. You don't have to clear them yourself first — they just come off your net.
