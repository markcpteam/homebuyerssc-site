## Quick Answer

You can sell an inherited house in South Carolina once probate has opened and the executor (or personal representative) has legal authority to act. Depending on the will, the type of probate, and how many heirs are involved, the process can take anywhere from a few weeks to several months. Selling for cash is often the fastest path because you skip repairs, showings, and the wait for a financed buyer. Most inherited homes are sold as-is — heirs rarely want to invest in a property they don't plan to keep. ## How to Sell an Inherited Home in SC

  1. **Open probate (if it isn't already).** File the will with the county Probate Court where the decedent lived. If there's no will, the court will appoint a personal representative under SC's intestate succession laws.

  2. **Confirm your authority to sell.** Once the court issues Letters Testamentary (with a will) or Letters of Administration (without one), you have the legal authority to act on behalf of the estate. The closing attorney will need a copy.

  3. **Get the home valued.** Order an appraisal or comparative market analysis. This establishes the property's fair market value at the date of death — important for the step-up in cost basis and for evaluating offers.

  4. **Decide as-is or repaired.** For most heirs, as-is wins. The math rarely justifies spending estate funds on cosmetic work, and the home almost always needs more than a coat of paint.

  5. **Get all heirs aligned.** If multiple heirs share the property, you'll need everyone to agree on price, buyer, and timing before going under contract.

  6. **Choose your sale path.** Cash buyer if you want speed and simplicity, MLS if the home is updated and you have time to wait. Many estates pick cash because they're managing the property remotely.

  7. **Close through the attorney and distribute proceeds.** The closing attorney coordinates title work, deed transfer, and the wire of funds. The estate receives the proceeds, which are then distributed per the will (or by intestacy).

## Common Mistakes Heirs Make

  * **Trying to sell before probate is opened.** No matter how clear the intent, you can't legally transfer title until the court has granted authority.

  * **Moving forward without all heirs in agreement.** One holdout can stall a sale indefinitely. Get consensus first.

  * **Spending estate money on repairs.** Most updates return 50–70 cents on the dollar. For a property nobody plans to keep, that's a losing trade.

  * **Overlooking the step-up in basis.** Inherited property gets a new cost basis equal to the date-of-death value. This usually eliminates or sharply reduces capital gains on a sale soon after.

  * **Forgetting ongoing carrying costs.** Every month the property sits, the estate keeps paying taxes, insurance, utilities, and (if applicable) HOA fees.

  * **Missing the homestead exemption change.** The deceased's property tax exemption ends with their death. Reassessment can bump the tax bill considerably.

  * **Choosing a buyer who doesn't understand probate.** A buyer unfamiliar with the process can panic at delays that are completely routine.

## Frequently Asked Questions

## Can I sell an inherited house before probate is complete?

You can go under contract once the court issues Letters Testamentary or Letters of Administration. You usually can't close until probate has progressed far enough to authorize the sale — typically a few weeks to a few months in.

## Do all the heirs have to agree to sell?

If multiple heirs share the property, yes. One heir can't force a sale unilaterally without a partition action through the courts — which is slow and expensive.

## Do I have to pay capital gains tax on an inherited house?

Inherited property gets a "step-up in basis" to its date-of-death value. If you sell soon after at roughly that value, capital gains are usually minimal. Consult a tax professional for your specific situation.

## What if the house has a mortgage on it?

The mortgage gets paid off from the sale proceeds at closing. Any remaining equity flows to the estate.

## What if the deceased had outstanding debts?

Creditors of the estate are paid from estate assets, which can include the sale proceeds, before distributions to heirs. The probate process handles this in a defined order under SC law.

## How long does probate take in South Carolina?

A simple, uncontested probate typically runs 8 to 12 months. Smaller estates may qualify for summary administration, which is faster. Contested estates can take years.

## Should I clean out the house before selling?

Not for a cash sale. Cash buyers regularly purchase homes with furniture and belongings still in place. For a traditional listing, yes — you'll need to clean out and stage.

## Can a cash buyer help with the cleanout?

Most do. You can leave whatever you don't want, and the buyer handles disposal after closing. That alone can save heirs days of work and significant moving costs.
