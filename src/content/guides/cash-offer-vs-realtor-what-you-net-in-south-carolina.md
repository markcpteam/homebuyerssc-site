A listing advertises a higher price. A cash offer is a lower number with less taken out of it and a much higher chance of actually happening. Which one nets you more depends on your house, your timeline, and what a listing would genuinely cost you, so the only honest answer is to run both sets of numbers for your own property.

What we can give you is our side of it, measured rather than asserted, next to the national figures for listed sales.

**Homebuyers SC closes 98% of the purchase contracts it signs.** Nationally, 7% of contracts were terminated in the three months to August 2026, and 14% had delayed settlements.

## How often does each kind of sale actually close?

This is the comparison that matters most, and it is the one sellers almost never run. A contract is not a closing.

| | Listed sale, national | Homebuyers SC |
|---|---|---|
| Contracts that do not close | 7% terminated in the three months to August 2026 | 2% |
| Settlements delayed | 14%, with 6% delayed by appraisal issues | Not tracked separately, see timeline below |
| Why deals fail | Buyer financing, appraisal, inspection renegotiation, buyer's own house not selling | Title problems, or a major undiscovered issue with the house |
| Who decides | The buyer's lender | No lender is involved |

The reason for the gap is structural, not effort. A listed sale depends on a stranger's mortgage approval, an appraiser's opinion, and an inspection that can reopen the price. A cash purchase has none of those. When a Homebuyers SC contract does not close, it is because the title work turned up something or the house turned out to have a problem nobody knew about, not because financing fell through.

Note what that 7% costs a seller. A terminated contract does not just mean starting over. It means starting over having already paid the carrying costs, with a house that now shows as having been under contract and come back.

## How long does each one take?

| | Listed sale | Homebuyers SC |
|---|---|---|
| Before an offer | However long the house sits on the market | Usually 24 to 48 hours after a walkthrough |
| After an offer is accepted | 30 to 45 days for a lender to underwrite and fund, when nothing goes wrong | No lender. Once title is clear, usually one to three weeks |
| If you need longer | Not really your choice, the lender sets the pace | Homebuyers SC will set a closing date up to **12 months** out if that is what you need |
| If something goes wrong | Delay, renegotiation, or termination | Title work is the usual cause of delay |

The 12-month figure is the one worth dwelling on, because it is the opposite of what people expect from a cash buyer. Sellers who need time, for a tenant to find housing, for a probate step, for a school year to end, for a new house to be ready, can have it. The speed is available when you want it. It is not a requirement.

## How do I work out what a listing would actually net me?

Do this before comparing anything. The gross sale price is not the number to compare.

1. **Start with a realistic sale price**, not the aspirational list price. Ask the agent for recent comparable sales, not an opinion.
2. **Subtract commission.** There is no standard rate. Commission is not set by law and is fully negotiable, and since the 2024 National Association of Realtors settlement, compensation to a buyer's agent is no longer published through the MLS and is negotiated separately. Get the figure in writing from each agent you interview, and ask separately whether you are being asked to contribute toward the buyer's agent.
3. **Subtract concessions.** Closing cost help and post-inspection credits are common. Ask the agent what recent comparable sales in your area actually included, since they can see that data and you cannot.
4. **Subtract repairs.** Both what you spend before listing and what the inspection reopens afterward.
5. **Subtract holding costs.** Your mortgage, property taxes, insurance, utilities, and any HOA dues, multiplied by how long you realistically expect to be on the market and in escrow. Use your own monthly number.
6. **Apply the risk.** 7% of contracts terminate. If yours does, you have spent everything above and are back at the start.
7. **Compare the two nets.** A cash offer has none of the subtractions in steps 2 through 5. The number on the offer, minus your mortgage payoff and any liens, is what you receive.

## When is listing the better choice?

Often, and any buyer who tells you otherwise is selling you something.

**List the house if** it is in good condition, a lender would finance it today, you have time to wait, you can fund repairs and carrying costs, and there is nothing about your situation forcing a deadline. The higher gross price will usually survive the subtractions and beat a cash offer. That is not a close call, and Homebuyers SC will tell you so.

**A cash sale usually wins if** any of these apply:

- The house needs work a lender will not finance, so retail buyers cannot buy it regardless of price. See [selling a house no one will finance](/guides/selling-a-house-no-one-will-finance-your-best-options/).
- You are against a deadline: a foreclosure sale date, a relocation, an estate that needs to close. See [stopping foreclosure in South Carolina](/guides/stopping-foreclosure-in-south-carolina-options-timeline/).
- You cannot fund repairs or carrying costs while it sits. See [do I need repairs before selling as-is](/guides/do-i-need-repairs-before-selling-as-is-an-sc-sellers-guide/).
- The house is occupied by a tenant, full of belongings, or in probate, which narrows the retail buyer pool badly. See [selling an inherited house in probate in South Carolina](/guides/selling-an-inherited-house-in-probate-in-south-carolina/).
- A previous listing already failed. That is information, and it usually means the house needs something you were not prepared to give it.

## What actually gets deducted from a cash offer?

Nothing, on a straightforward purchase, beyond what you already owe on the house.

A cash offer is below what a renovated house would fetch on the open market. That gap is the trade: speed, no repairs, no showings, no commission, and a sale that closes. What should not happen is further deductions appearing later. Ask any buyer to put in writing what comes out of the offer before you sign, and ask them to show the arithmetic behind the number: what they think the house is worth repaired, what they estimate the repairs cost, and what margin they are keeping. A buyer who will not show that is asking you to trust a figure you cannot check. See [how to choose a cash home buyer in Charleston](/how-to-choose-a-cash-home-buyer/).

In South Carolina the closing must be supervised by a licensed attorney. That is settled law from *State v. Buyers Service Co.*, 292 S.C. 426, 357 S.E.2d 15 (1987), not local custom, and it means there is an independent professional handling the payoffs and the title work on every sale in this state.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a cash offer lower than market value?**

Yes, and any buyer who says otherwise is not being straight with you. It is below what a renovated house lists for. The comparison worth making is not against that number, it is against what a listing would leave you with after commission, concessions, repairs, carrying costs, and a 7% chance of not closing at all.

**What is the real estate commission in South Carolina?**

There is no set rate. It is not set by law and is fully negotiable. Ask each agent for their figure in writing.

**Can I list the house and consider cash offers at the same time?**

Depends on what your listing agreement says. Read it, or ask the agent before you sign it. Some sellers keep a cash offer as a floor while the house is listed.

**Is the cash offer negotiable?**

Yes. If you have a competing offer, or information about the house the buyer has not seen, bring it. A real buyer will look at it.

**Do cash buyers pay closing costs?**

On a straightforward purchase Homebuyers SC covers standard closing costs including the attorney's fee, and there is no commission. Get any buyer's answer to this in writing rather than assuming it.

**How fast can it close?**

Homebuyers SC has closed purchases in as few as 35 days from first contact to funds in hand. Within that, once the closing attorney confirms clear title, the closing itself is usually one to three weeks away. If you need longer, say so, and the date is set to suit you up to twelve months out. See [how fast can I sell my house in South Carolina](/guides/how-fast-can-i-sell-my-house-in-south-carolina-timeline-guide/).

**What happens in the 2% that do not close?**

Title problems, or something significant about the house that nobody knew at the time of contract. Both are found by the closing attorney or during due diligence rather than sprung at the last minute. See [selling a house with title problems](/guides/selling-a-house-with-title-problems-how-to-close-when-ownership-isnt-clear/).
