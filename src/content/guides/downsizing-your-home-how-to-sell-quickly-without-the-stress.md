Downsizing usually stalls on two things, and neither of them is the house being hard to sell.

The first is the contents of a house lived in for decades. The second is a repair list that has to be dealt with before a lender will finance it for a retail buyer. Both are solvable, and the cheapest solution is often not the one people reach for first.

Homebuyers SC buys houses across Charleston, Berkeley, and Dorchester counties with the contents inside and no repairs required.

## What actually makes this hard?

- **The contents.** Decades of belongings, and the sorting is slow and emotionally heavy.
- **Deferred maintenance.** Things that were fine while you lived there and are not fine for an appraiser.
- **The sequencing.** Selling this house and buying or moving into the next one, in an order that does not leave you homeless or paying twice.
- **Physical capacity.** Packing, clearing, and managing contractors is a large job.
- **Nobody local to help**, if adult children live elsewhere.

Naming which of these is actually your obstacle is worth doing, because they have different answers.

## Will my property taxes change?

Probably, and it is worth knowing before you move rather than after.

Under SC Code 12-43-220, an owner-occupied legal residence is assessed at **4%** of fair market value and other real property at **6%**. A house you no longer occupy as your legal residence generally does not qualify for the 4% ratio, so moving out and leaving it empty can increase the tax bill while you are also paying for somewhere else.

There is a specific exception worth knowing for anyone moving into care rather than into a smaller home. If an owner entitled to the 4% ratio "becomes a patient at a nursing home or a community residential care facility," the statute allows them to retain that ratio for as long as they remain there, provided they otherwise qualify, intend to return, and the property is not rented beyond what the statute allows. See [selling a parent's house after a move to assisted living](/guides/selling-a-house-when-an-aging-owner-moves-to-assisted-living/).

*Assessment questions depend on your circumstances. Confirm with the county assessor, and with a tax professional where it matters.*

## Do I have to clear the house first?

No. This is the part that changes the whole project.

A cash buyer takes the house with the contents inside, and disposal becomes their expense. What you do is take what you want and leave the rest. For a house lived in for thirty years, that difference is measured in months.

| | Clear it, repair it, list it | Sell as-is with contents |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Clean-out, then repairs, then staging | None |
| Who sorts everything | You | You take what you want, the rest transfers |
| Time | Months | 1 to 3 weeks, or a date you choose |
| Showings | Many, while you are trying to move | Usually one walkthrough |
| Price | Higher gross, after two rounds of spending | Lower, and net of everything else often close |

See [selling an inherited house full of belongings](/guides/selling-a-property-after-inheriting-a-messy-estate-how-to-simplify-the-chaos/) and [selling a hoarder house without cleaning it out](/guides/selling-a-hoarder-house-how-to-sell-without-cleaning-everything-out/) if the house is genuinely full.

## How do I sequence this against buying the next place?

This is the practical problem, and it is where a set closing date earns its value.

- **A written offer with a firm date** gives you a number and a deadline to plan the next move around, which a listing cannot.
- **A closing date you choose** means you are not moving twice. Homebuyers SC will set a closing up to twelve months out.
- **Avoid carrying two places**, which is what happens when a listing runs long or a contract fails. Nationally, 7% of contracts were terminated in the three months to August 2026.

If the house is in good condition, a lender would finance it today, and you have time, **listing is usually the better answer** and the higher price survives the costs. The arithmetic is in [cash offer vs listing with an agent in Charleston](/guides/cash-offer-vs-realtor-what-you-net-in-south-carolina/).

## What if I can no longer keep up with the house?

That is a common and sufficient reason on its own, and it tends to arrive alongside a repair list that grew while the house was getting harder to manage.

The honest question is not whether you should have kept up with it. It is whether the accumulated work is bounded or not. If a lender would still finance it, you have options. If not, a cash sale is the route that does not require you to project-manage a renovation you did not want. See [do I need repairs before selling as-is](/guides/do-i-need-repairs-before-selling-as-is-an-sc-sellers-guide/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I stay until I find somewhere?**

Yes. The closing date can be set to suit the move, and a post-closing possession arrangement can sometimes be negotiated. Raise it early.

**What happens to what I leave behind?**

It transfers with the house. Take documents, photographs, and anything of personal or financial value first, and set a real date for that.

**Should I move out before selling?**

Not necessarily, and moving out can raise the tax bill and complicate the insurance while adding a second housing cost.

**What if adult children want things from the house?**

Set one date, tell everyone, and let them come. It is far easier than coordinating over months.

**Will an empty house sell better?**

It photographs better for a listing. For a cash sale it makes no difference at all.

**How fast can it close?**

Usually one to three weeks once the closing attorney confirms clear title, or later if that suits. In South Carolina that supervision is required under *State v. Buyers Service Co.*, 292 S.C. 426, 357 S.E.2d 15 (1987).
