You can sell a South Carolina rental with a non-paying tenant still in it, and you do not have to complete the eviction first. An investor buyer prices the situation and takes it on after closing. For many landlords that is faster and cheaper than running the eviction themselves and then marketing a vacant house.

Homebuyers SC buys occupied rentals across Charleston, Berkeley, and Dorchester counties, including properties with tenants who have stopped paying.

## What are my legal options when a tenant stops paying in SC?

Two things matter most, and one of them is what you cannot do.

Under SC Code 27-40-710(B), the landlord gives written notice allowing the tenant **five days from the date due** to pay before the rental agreement can be terminated. South Carolina adds a wrinkle worth knowing: if the written lease already contains the required notice language, no separate notice is needed before starting eviction proceedings.

What you cannot do is handle it yourself. **Self-help eviction is illegal in South Carolina.** Changing the locks, removing a door, shutting off the power or water, or hauling belongings to the curb exposes you to liability no matter how much rent is owed. Eviction runs through magistrate's court, and until the court acts, the tenant is lawfully in possession.

*The statutes cited here are the starting point, not the whole answer. How they apply turns on your specific facts, so confirm with a licensed South Carolina attorney before acting.*

| Option | What it costs you | Realistic timing |
|---|---|---|
| Serve notice and file for eviction | Filing fees, likely attorney fees, your time | Depends on the magistrate's docket, and the tenant can contest |
| Cash for keys | A negotiated payment, in writing | Often the fastest route to vacancy, when the tenant agrees |
| Payment plan | Nothing upfront, but the arrears may never be recovered | Works only when the tenant's problem is temporary |
| Sell occupied to an investor | A lower price reflecting the situation | Usually 1 to 3 weeks |

The arithmetic that decides it: every month you spend evicting is a month of mortgage, taxes, insurance, and no rent, followed by turnover costs on a unit a frustrated tenant just left.

## Why do most retail buyers disappear?

Because a lender needs an appraisal and an inspection, and a hostile occupant makes both difficult or impossible to schedule. Owner-occupant buyers want possession at closing and you cannot promise it.

That leaves investors, who are used to it. The property is still an asset, it is just an asset with a problem attached, and the price reflects the cost of solving that problem.

## How does a buyer price a non-paying tenant?

Openly, if the buyer is any good. The offer accounts for the estimated cost and time to resolve occupancy, plus whatever condition issues come with it.

What helps your price:

- **Documentation.** The lease, the payment history, the notices you have sent, and any court filings. A buyer who knows exactly where things stand prices the known. A buyer guessing prices the worst case.
- **Honesty about the tenant.** Whether they are communicating, whether there is a dispute about habitability, whether the arrears are two months or fourteen.
- **The security deposit ledger.** It transfers at closing and it has to be right. Under SC Code 27-40-410 a landlord who wrongfully withholds a deposit faces three times the amount plus attorney's fees, and that obligation follows the money, not the address.

What hurts it: discovering any of the above after the contract is signed. A buyer who is surprised renegotiates.

## Should I evict first, or sell as-is?

| | Evict first, then sell | Sell occupied |
|---|---|---|
| Cash out of pocket | Filing and attorney fees, plus continued carrying costs | None |
| Time before you have money | Eviction, then turnover, then a listing period | 1 to 3 weeks |
| Condition risk | A unit vacated under duress often needs work | Buyer's problem |
| Price | Higher on a clean, vacant house | Lower, reflecting the situation |
| Certainty | The tenant can contest and extend it | Closing date is set |

Evicting first makes sense when the arrears are small, the tenant is cooperative about leaving, and the house is otherwise in good shape. When the tenancy has genuinely broken down, the eviction is usually the most expensive part of a plan to sell.

## What if the property has other problems too?

Common, and it does not change the answer, only the price. Deferred maintenance because the rent stopped covering it. Unpaid utilities, which in South Carolina can be a lien on the property superior to everything but taxes. Code enforcement cases on a property nobody has been maintaining.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to finish the eviction before selling?**

No. The buyer can take the property with the tenant in place and handle it. Tell them exactly where the process stands.

**Can I just change the locks?**

No. Self-help eviction is illegal in South Carolina and creates liability regardless of how much is owed.

**Does the lease transfer to the buyer?**

Yes. An existing lease binds the new owner, including a lease the tenant is currently breaching. The buyer inherits both the lease and the claim for unpaid rent.

**Can I keep the unpaid rent claim?**

Negotiable, and it should be written into the contract rather than assumed. Most investor buyers do not want it and will leave it with you.

**Will I get much less for the property?**

Less than a clean vacant sale, yes. Compare it against the cost of the eviction, the lost rent during it, the turnover afterward, and a listing period, not against a best-case sale that is several months away.

**How fast can it close?**

Usually one to three weeks once the closing attorney confirms clear title. See [how fast can I sell my house in South Carolina](/guides/how-fast-can-i-sell-my-house-in-south-carolina-timeline-guide/).

**What if I inherited the property with the tenant already there?**

Probate and the lease run on separate clocks. See [selling an inherited house with tenants still inside](/guides/selling-an-inherited-house-with-tenants-still-inside/).
