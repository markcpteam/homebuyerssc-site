## Quick Answer

You can sell a tenant-occupied home in South Carolina — the lease stays with the property and transfers to the new owner at closing. The buyer pool does narrow, though, because most owner-occupants want a vacant home, while investors and cash buyers will purchase with tenants in place. Your job as the seller is to honor the lease, give proper notice for showings, and provide clean documentation (lease agreement, rent roll, security deposit records). Selling with tenants is often faster and easier than waiting for a vacancy — especially when you're working with experienced [cash home buyers in West Ashley](https://homebuyerssc.com/cash-home-buyers-west-ashley-sc/) who routinely purchase occupied rentals as-is.

## How to Sell a Tenant-Occupied Home

  1. **Review the lease and confirm your obligations.** Note the end date, any early-termination clauses, notice requirements, and whether the lease is month-to-month or fixed-term. A fixed-term lease binds the new owner; a month-to-month gives both parties more flexibility.

  2. **Decide whether to sell occupied or wait for vacancy.** If the lease has months or years left, you'll almost certainly need to sell to an investor. If the tenant is month-to-month or near the end of a lease, you can choose to wait.

  3. **Notify your tenants in writing.** South Carolina doesn't require a specific "intent to sell" notice, but giving tenants a heads-up — and addressing their concerns about showings, deposits, and lease continuity — keeps the relationship workable.

  4. **Gather the paperwork.** You'll need the signed lease, a current rent roll, a security deposit ledger, and any repair or maintenance records. Buyers (and their lawyers) will ask for all of it.

  5. **Choose the right buyer.** Investor buyers and cash home buyers are comfortable with occupied properties. Listing on the MLS with tenants in place is possible but limits you to a smaller pool of buyers willing to honor the lease.

  6. **Coordinate showings respectfully.** South Carolina law requires reasonable notice (commonly 24 hours) before entry for a non-emergency reason like a showing.

  7. **Transfer the lease and security deposit at closing.** The closing attorney will handle the prorations for rent and credit the security deposit to the new owner, who becomes responsible for returning it at the end of the lease.

## Common Mistakes Landlord-Sellers Make

  * **Trying to evict in order to sell.** Evicting a paying tenant just to vacate the property usually doesn't hold up legally and takes longer than just selling occupied.

  * **Showing the property without proper notice.** Walking buyers through unannounced creates legal exposure and damages the relationship with a tenant whose cooperation you need.

  * **Pressuring tenants for a quick move-out.** Cash-for-keys offers can work, but threatening or pushing without legal grounds invites trouble.

  * **Hiding lease terms from buyers.** Buyers will request the lease during due diligence. Surface any unusual terms upfront — month-to-month conversions, below-market rent, or open repair issues.

  * **Forgetting the security deposit transfer.** The deposit belongs to the tenant. If you keep it instead of crediting the new owner, you'll be liable when it's time to return it.

  * **Listing as "delivered vacant" when you can't actually deliver it.** If the lease runs past closing, the property is not vacant. Misrepresenting that kills deals and invites lawsuits.

  * **Failing to vet the buyer's intent.** Some buyers plan to terminate or non-renew immediately. If you care how your tenants are treated, ask.

## Frequently Asked Questions

## Can I sell my house if a tenant is on a lease?

Yes. The lease transfers with the property to the new owner, who must honor its terms until the lease expires.

## Does the lease automatically transfer to the new owner?

Yes. In South Carolina, an existing lease binds the new owner. The tenant keeps their right to occupy under the original terms.

## Can I evict my tenants just to sell the house?

Generally no. Without a legal cause (non-payment, lease violation, etc.) or a month-to-month termination, you can't evict simply because you want to sell.

## How much notice do I have to give for showings?

South Carolina law requires reasonable notice for non-emergency entry. Twenty-four hours is the standard, and many leases spell out the exact requirement.

## What happens to the security deposit at closing?

It's credited from seller to buyer at closing, and the new owner becomes responsible for returning it (or applying it) at the end of the lease.

## Do tenants have a right of first refusal to buy the property?

Only if their lease explicitly grants one. Otherwise, no — you can sell to anyone.

## Can I sell a property with a problem tenant?

Yes. A cash investor will usually buy a property with a difficult tenant in place. Be transparent about the situation, and the buyer can decide how to proceed after closing.

## Will I get less for the property because it's tenanted?

Sometimes, especially if rent is well below market or the tenant has many months left. But for landlords selling to other investors, an occupied property with paying tenants can actually be more valuable — it's already producing income on day one.
