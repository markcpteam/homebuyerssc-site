## Quick Answer

You can sell a tenant-occupied house in South Carolina. The lease stays with the property and binds the new owner at closing. What changes is the buyer pool: owner-occupants want vacant possession, so a tenant in place usually means selling to an investor or cash buyer.

Your obligations do not change because you are selling. Honor the lease, give reasonable notice before entry, and get the security deposit accounting right, because South Carolina attaches triple damages to that last one.

Homebuyers SC buys tenant-occupied houses across Charleston, West Ashley, Mount Pleasant, Summerville, and the surrounding Lowcountry, with no vacancy required.

## What are the South Carolina rules a landlord-seller has to work within?

These come from the South Carolina Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, Title 27, Chapter 40. They apply whether or not you are selling.

| Situation | What South Carolina requires | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| Tenant has not paid rent | Written notice giving the tenant 5 days from the date due to pay. If the lease already contains the required notice language, no separate notice is needed before starting eviction | 27-40-710(B) |
| Material lease violation other than nonpayment | Written notice specifying the breach, terminating on a date "not less than fourteen days after receipt" if not cured in 14 days | 27-40-710(A) |
| Ending a month-to-month tenancy | Written notice at least 30 days before the termination date | 27-40-770 |
| Ending a week-to-week tenancy | Written notice at least 7 days before | 27-40-770 |
| Security deposit after tenancy ends | Return it, or provide an itemized list of damages, within 30 days after termination and delivery of possession and demand, whichever is later | 27-40-410 |
| Getting the deposit wrong | The tenant may recover "three times the amount wrongfully withheld and reasonable attorney's fees" | 27-40-410 |

The deposit rule is the one that bites landlords during a sale, because the deposit gets forgotten in the closing paperwork and then somebody is holding money they are not entitled to hold.

There is no self-help eviction in South Carolina. Changing locks, removing doors, or shutting off utilities to force a tenant out is not an option regardless of how far behind they are. Eviction runs through magistrate's court.

*The statutes cited here are the starting point, not the whole answer. How they apply turns on your specific facts, so confirm with a licensed South Carolina attorney before acting.*

## How do I sell a tenant-occupied house?

1. **Read the lease first.** End date, early termination clauses, notice requirements, and whether it is fixed-term or month-to-month. A fixed term binds the buyer for its remaining life. A month-to-month gives everyone options.
2. **Decide: sell occupied, or wait for vacancy.** Months or years left on a fixed term means selling to an investor. Month-to-month, or close to the end, gives you a real choice.
3. **Tell the tenant in writing.** South Carolina does not require a specific notice of intent to sell, but a tenant who learns about it from a stranger with a lockbox becomes an obstacle. You need their cooperation for access.
4. **Gather the paperwork.** Signed lease, current rent roll, security deposit ledger, and repair records. The buyer and the closing attorney will ask for all of it.
5. **Pick a buyer who actually buys occupied property.** Listing on the MLS with a tenant in place is possible and narrows your pool considerably.
6. **Give proper notice before showings.** Reasonable notice for non-emergency entry, commonly 24 hours, and many leases specify it exactly.
7. **Transfer the lease and deposit at closing.** The closing attorney prorates rent and credits the security deposit to the buyer, who then owns the obligation to return it.

## What goes wrong most often?

- **Trying to evict in order to sell.** Wanting the property vacant is not legal cause. Without nonpayment, a lease violation, or a properly noticed month-to-month termination, it does not work, and it takes longer than simply selling occupied.
- **Showing the house without notice.** Creates exposure and destroys the cooperation you need.
- **Pressuring a tenant to leave.** Cash for keys, negotiated in writing, is legitimate. Pressure without legal grounds is not.
- **Forgetting the deposit at closing.** Triple damages plus attorney's fees. Put it on the settlement statement.
- **Listing as delivered vacant when the lease runs past closing.** The property is not vacant. This kills deals late and invites claims.
- **Hiding lease terms.** Below-market rent, an option to purchase, an unresolved repair obligation. Buyers find all of it in due diligence. Disclose early and the price adjusts. Disclose late and the deal dies.

## When does selling occupied make more sense than waiting?

| | Wait for vacancy, then list | Sell occupied to an investor |
|---|---|---|
| Time | The remaining lease term, plus turnover, plus listing period | Usually 1 to 3 weeks |
| Lost rent | Vacancy during turnover and marketing | None, rent continues to closing |
| Turnover cost | Paint, flooring, cleaning, repairs | None |
| Buyer pool | Owner-occupants and investors | Investors and cash buyers |
| Effect of a difficult tenant | You deal with it first | The buyer takes it on |
| Price | Potentially higher if the house shows well | Lower, but net of everything above it is often close |

A paying tenant is not always a discount. To an investor buyer, an occupied property producing income on day one can be worth more than a vacant one, particularly when the rent is at or near market.

Where the discount shows up is a long lease at well below market rent, or a tenant situation the buyer has to resolve. ## Frequently Asked Questions

## Can I sell my house if a tenant is on a lease?

Yes. The lease transfers with the property and the new owner must honor its terms until it expires.

## Does the lease automatically transfer to the new owner?

Yes. An existing lease binds the buyer in South Carolina. The tenant keeps the right to occupy under the original terms, including the original rent.

## Can I evict my tenants just so I can sell?

No. Wanting to sell is not legal cause. You would need nonpayment, a material lease violation with proper notice, or a month-to-month termination on 30 days written notice under 27-40-770.

## How much notice do I have to give for showings?

Reasonable notice for non-emergency entry. Twenty-four hours is the working standard and many leases state it explicitly. Check the lease before assuming.

## What happens to the security deposit at closing?

It is credited from seller to buyer on the settlement statement, and the new owner becomes responsible for returning or applying it. Getting this wrong exposes you to three times the amount wrongfully withheld plus attorney's fees under 27-40-410.

## Do tenants have a right of first refusal to buy?

Only if the lease grants one. Read the lease, because an option to purchase buried in a renewal addendum will stop a sale cold.

## Can I sell with a tenant who has stopped paying?

Yes, and you do not have to complete the eviction first. Be straightforward about the situation. The buyer prices it and decides how to handle it after closing. See [selling a house after a tenant stops paying rent](/guides/selling-a-house-after-a-tenant-stops-paying-rent/).

## What if the property was inherited and came with a tenant?

Two separate timelines running at once, probate and the lease. See [selling an inherited house with tenants still inside](/guides/selling-an-inherited-house-with-tenants-still-inside/).

## How fast can a sale with tenants close?

Usually one to three weeks once the closing attorney confirms clear title, same as any cash sale. The lease documentation is what most often adds a day or two. See [how fast can I sell my house in South Carolina](/guides/how-fast-can-i-sell-my-house-in-south-carolina-timeline-guide/).
